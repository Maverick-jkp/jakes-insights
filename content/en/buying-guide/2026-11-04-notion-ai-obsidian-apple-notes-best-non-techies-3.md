Apple Notes wins for non-techies. That's the short version.

It's free, it's instant, and in real-world 30-day testing, users generated 14.7 notes per day — more than Notion and Obsidian combined. For anyone who isn't maintaining a knowledge graph or running a distributed team, it's the obvious default.

But that's not the whole story. If you're a consultant juggling five active client projects, Notion's Kanban boards and real-time collaboration justify the $10/month Plus tier. And if you're a solo researcher who thinks in connections rather than lists, Obsidian's bidirectional linking earns its learning curve.

This comparison covers four dimensions: **capture speed**, **organization overhead**, **AI capabilities**, and **data portability**.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - **Apple Notes:** Zero friction, zero cost — the right default for anyone inside the Apple ecosystem
> - **Notion:** Built for teams needing collaboration, project tracking, and database-style workflows
> - **Obsidian:** Ideal for solo knowledge workers who want local file ownership and don't mind setup
> - **None of the above:** If end-to-end encryption is non-negotiable by default, look at Standard Notes instead

## The Contenders

**Apple Notes** ships free with every Apple device. The 2025–2026 update cycle added Smart Folders, improved Apple Pencil OCR, and on-device AI summarization via Apple Intelligence. It's not a power tool — it's a fast, invisible capture layer that stays out of your way. Lock-screen swipe access puts a note 15 seconds from your pocket to text.

The tradeoffs are real: no Markdown, no bulk export without third-party tools, and it's Apple-only. No Windows. No Android.

**Obsidian** (v1.13.0 as of 2026) stores every note as a plain Markdown file on your local drive. Free for personal use; Sync costs $5/month. The 1,800+ community plugins mean you can build almost any workflow — but "can build" is doing heavy lifting in that sentence. Version 1.9.0 added native database functionality (Bases), closing the gap with Notion for structured data. According to [The Modern Observer's 30-day benchmark](https://themodernobserver.com/tech/notion-vs-obsidian-vs-apple-notes-2026), Obsidian users averaged 11.4 notes per day with an 8-second retrieval average.

**Notion** ($10/month Plus, $18/user/month Business, $10/month AI add-on) is a block-based workspace that converts pages into filterable databases. For teams, it's genuinely excellent — real-time collaboration, Kanban, timelines, permissions. The cost: according to [stik.ink's 2026 Mac app comparison](https://www.stik.ink/blog/best-note-taking-app-for-mac-2026), new notes take 4–6 seconds minimum to load, offline access is unreliable, and the free tier became meaningfully limited after Notion's 2025 price increase.

*Sources: [The Modern Observer 30-day benchmark](https://themodernobserver.com/tech/notion-vs-obsidian-vs-apple-notes-2026), [stik.ink 2026 comparison](https://www.stik.ink/blog/best-note-taking-app-for-mac-2026), vendor pricing pages as of November 2026.*

**The organization overhead gap is the real story.** Notion users spent 35% of their note-taking time on organization — more than four times Apple Notes' 8%. That's not a preference difference. It's a tax. Every note triggers a decision: which database does this belong to? What properties should I set? For non-techies, that overhead compounds fast.

**Obsidian's retrieval speed advantage comes down to architecture.** Local indexing skips server round-trips entirely. The 8-second average versus Apple Notes' 15 seconds reflects the difference between querying a local SQLite index and hitting a cloud API. For researchers working from a large vault, that gap widens considerably.

**The AI cost comparison isn't close.** Apple Intelligence summarization runs on-device at no extra charge. Notion AI's workspace-wide Q&A costs $10/month on top of an already paid subscription. Obsidian's bring-your-own-key approach via Claude or GPT plugins is free if you have an existing API key — but that's a technical setup step that excludes most non-techies by definition.

**Data portability is Obsidian's structural advantage.** Plain Markdown files mean you can migrate to Bear, iA Writer, or VS Code in minutes. Notion's export breaks relational database structures — linked databases, formulas, and relations don't survive the transfer. That's not a minor inconvenience. It's lock-in.

## Where Each One Actually Breaks

**Apple Notes breaks when you cross the Apple wall.** One Android device on your team and the whole system falls apart. No web app for non-Apple users, no sharing with Windows collaborators, and bulk export depends on third-party tools that vary wildly in reliability. According to [stik.ink's comparison](https://www.stik.ink/blog/best-note-taking-app-for-mac-2026), migration away from Apple Notes is described as "painful." If there's any chance your workflow needs to leave the Apple ecosystem, that pain is already baked in.

**Obsidian breaks under plugin dependency.** The 1,800+ plugin ecosystem is a selling point until a core plugin stops being maintained — which happens regularly in open-source communities. Workflows built on third-party plugins can break silently after an Obsidian update. [The Modern Observer's test](https://themodernobserver.com/tech/notion-vs-obsidian-vs-apple-notes-2026) flagged 15% time spent on organization, but that number climbs sharply once you're managing a plugin stack. For non-techies, a broken plugin with no support path is simply a dead end.

**Notion breaks offline.** Its cloud-first architecture means unreliable access without an internet connection — [stik.ink explicitly flags this](https://www.stik.ink/blog/best-note-taking-app-for-mac-2026) as a known 2026 limitation. On a flight, in a rural area, or during an outage, Notion can lock you out of your own notes. The 4–6 second load time per new note gets worse in low-bandwidth conditions.

## The Verdict

**Apple Notes wins for non-techies in 2026.** The data holds up across every dimension that matters for most people: fastest capture, lowest organization overhead, zero cost, and free on-device AI. The single meaningful constraint is Apple-only availability — if that's not an issue for your workflow, there's no compelling reason to pay for anything else.

Notion earns its place for teams. Five or more people collaborating on projects, needing Kanban views and shared permissions — that's the use case where the $10/month Plus tier pays back quickly. Just account for the organizational overhead it introduces.

Obsidian is the right call for solo knowledge workers who want local file ownership and don't mind a steeper setup. That last part matters. "Doesn't mind setup" is exactly what separates most non-techies from Obsidian's actual target user.

**Your next 10 minutes:** If you're on Apple hardware, open Apple Notes, create a Smart Folder for your current active project, and run it for two weeks. No plugins, no configuration, no subscription. [According to Atlas Workspace's 2026 test cited by Jake's Insights](https://jakeinsight.com/buying-guide/2026-08-10-notion-obsidian-apple-notes-note-app-worth-paying/), only 12% of quick-capture notes ever become archive-worthy — which means most people are paying for complexity they never actually use.

**The open question worth watching:** Obsidian's Bases feature — native databases added in v1.9.0 — is directly challenging Notion's core differentiator. If Bases matures into a full collaboration layer with real-time editing by mid-2027, the team workflow argument for Notion gets significantly weaker. That's the matchup to track.
