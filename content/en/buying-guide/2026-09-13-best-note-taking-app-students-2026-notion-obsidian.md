Notion is free for college students. Obsidian costs $8/month if you want sync. Apple Notes costs nothing, ever.

That's the pricing summary. But pricing isn't really the question. The question is which one doesn't break under the specific pressure of your actual semester — group projects at midnight, lectures you're half-awake for, research papers due in 72 hours.

The answer depends entirely on what you're studying.

---

## Bottom Line Up Front

**Notion wins for most students.** The free Plus plan with a `.edu` email gives college students a genuine all-in-one workspace — notes, tasks, databases, templates — at zero cost. Nothing else at that price comes close for breadth.

Notion isn't the right call if you're a CS or STEM student who thinks in linked concepts, hates browser tabs, and wants files you actually own. Obsidian wins that fight. And if you're on Apple hardware, only need lecture notes, and won't spend 20 minutes configuring anything, Apple Notes is quietly better than its reputation suggests.

> **Key Takeaways**
> - Notion scores 4.6/5.0 on a college workload rubric and offers a free Plus plan with a `.edu` email — the strongest value entry point in 2026.
> - Obsidian is free for personal use, $8/month for sync, runs on every major platform including Linux, and supports 2,000+ community plugins as of mid-2026.
> - Apple Notes added smart folders, math note rendering, and Apple Intelligence summaries in 2024–2025 updates — raising its floor significantly for Apple-ecosystem students.
> - A 2024 meta-analysis (*Educational Psychology Review*, 24 studies, Hedges' g = 0.248) found handwritten notes correlated with higher course achievement than typed notes — worth considering before going all-in on any digital workflow.
> - Most students need zero paid tools in their first year. Payment is only justified at specific friction points: cross-course PDF synthesis, audio-synced lectures, or heavy collaboration needs.

## The Three Contenders

**Notion** is a block-based workspace built by Notion Labs, running on a continuous release cycle with no discrete version numbers. Students with a `.edu` email get the Plus plan free — normally $12/month. It's not really a note-taking app; it's a database tool that also handles notes. That distinction matters more than it sounds. Pages nest inside databases, databases connect to calendars and task boards, and templates for syllabi or study schedules take about three minutes to clone. According to [Atlas Workspace's 2026 college app evaluation](https://www.atlasworkspace.ai/blog/best-note-taking-apps-for-college-students), Notion scored 4.6/5.0 across six criteria including lecture capture, PDF handling, sync, and retrieval.

**Obsidian** is a local-first Markdown editor from Obsidian.md, free for personal use with an optional $8/month Sync add-on. Every note is a plain `.md` file on your own drive — no vendor lock-in, no account required to access your own work. Its graph view maps bidirectional links between notes, and the plugin ecosystem sits at 2,000+ extensions as of mid-2026. According to [Echoprysm's June 2026 guide](https://echoprysm.com/en/guides/best-note-taking-apps-for-students/), Obsidian is the primary recommendation for students who need plain-file ownership without strings attached. It scored 3.9/5.0 in Atlas's rubric — lower than Notion, primarily on collaboration and template availability.

**Apple Notes** ships free with every Apple device, no upgrade needed. The 2024–2025 iOS/macOS updates added smart folders, math note rendering, and Apple Intelligence summaries — features that would've cost money in competing apps a year ago. It searches instantly, requires zero configuration, and opens faster than either alternative. The ceiling is real, though: no databases, no bidirectional linking, no Windows access. It scored 3.5/5.0 in Atlas's evaluation, held back by limited retrieval and zero cross-platform reach.

**The pricing row is more nuanced than it looks.** Notion's free tier only hits parity with Obsidian and Apple Notes if you have a `.edu` email. Without one, the free Notion plan drops to limited blocks and no page history — a real constraint by midterm week. Obsidian's personal use is genuinely free with no strings; the $8/month Sync is optional if you're on a single device or use iCloud Drive as a workaround.

**The ecosystem gap between Obsidian and its competitors is wider than the table suggests.** 2,000+ plugins means students have built tools specifically for spaced repetition, citation management, LaTeX rendering, and daily journaling — all inside one app. Notion's 50+ native integrations are polished, but they're integrations, not extensions. Apple Notes has neither.

**Collaboration is where Notion pulls away from both.** Obsidian Sync is personal-only by design. Apple Notes shared folders work fine for two people but fall apart fast in group study contexts. Notion's real-time multiplayer, shared databases, and permission controls aren't matched by either competitor.

**Apple Notes winning on learning curve isn't a consolation prize — it's a real feature.** Students in high-volume lecture periods don't have time to configure plugins or build databases. Apple Notes opens, you type, it saves. That frictionlessness compounds in value at 8 AM on a Monday.

## Where Each One Actually Breaks

**Notion breaks when your internet does.** Offline mode exists but it's limited — changes made offline don't always sync cleanly, and complex database views can fail to load entirely without a connection. On a campus with patchy Wi-Fi or during travel, this is a genuine problem, not an edge case. Reddit's r/NoteTaking community consistently flags offline reliability as Notion's most-cited student complaint. The second failure mode is subtler: Notion rewards time spent building systems. According to Echoprysm's analysis, the "productivity theater" risk — configuring templates instead of actually studying — is highest in Notion and Obsidian. It's easy to spend three hours building a perfect semester dashboard and learn nothing.

**Obsidian breaks when you need to collaborate or share fast.** No built-in sharing link. No guest access. No commenting system. If a professor asks you to submit notes or a study group wants to co-edit a document, Obsidian has no answer. You'd export to PDF or paste into Google Docs — neither is a smooth workflow. The plugin ecosystem is also a double-edged situation: a community plugin that breaks after an Obsidian update can disable critical functionality mid-semester, and there's no vendor support to fix it. You're dependent on a volunteer maintainer who may have moved on.

**Apple Notes breaks the moment you leave Apple.** One Android phone. One Windows lab computer. One study partner on a non-Apple device. Apple Notes becomes inaccessible or read-only. The web interface at iCloud.com exists but it's stripped-down and unreliable for heavy use. The math notes and Apple Intelligence features also require recent hardware — older iPhones and iPads don't get the full feature set, which matters if you're not upgrading every two years.

## The Verdict

Notion wins for most students in 2026. The `.edu` free Plus plan, real-time collaboration, and template ecosystem score 4.6/5.0 for good reason — it's the only option that handles notes *and* project tracking *and* group work without a paid upgrade. That verdict holds for humanities, pre-med, law, and general undergrad workflows.

Obsidian wins for CS and STEM students who need to map relationships between concepts across multiple courses and want files that will outlast any app subscription. Its 3.9/5.0 score doesn't mean it's worse — it optimizes for a different constraint: knowledge ownership over collaboration convenience.

Apple Notes is the right call only if you're fully committed to the Apple ecosystem and won't collaborate outside it. That's not a compromise. It's a deliberate trade-off, and for the right student, it's the correct one.

**The practical next step:** If you have a `.edu` email, open Notion and clone the "Student OS" template. Under 10 minutes, working setup for this semester. If you're in CS and considering Obsidian, download it, create five notes from last week's lectures, add three bidirectional links. That exercise tells you within 30 minutes whether the mental model fits how you actually think.

One thing worth tracking: Apple Intelligence's note summarization is expanding to more device tiers through late 2026. If that feature reaches older hardware at scale, Apple Notes' gap with Notion on retrieval narrows significantly — and the free-tier calculus shifts again. The answer that's right today may not be right in twelve months.

Pick the tool that survives contact with your actual semester. That's the only test that matters.
