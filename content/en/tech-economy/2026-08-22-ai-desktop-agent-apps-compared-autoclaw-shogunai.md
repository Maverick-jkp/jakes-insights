## Why This Confusion Exists Right Now

The OpenClaw open-source framework hit a tipping point in late 2025. With 260,000+ GitHub stars and 500+ community-built skills, it became the de facto foundation for anyone building a desktop AI agent. That popularity created a Cambrian explosion of wrappers, forks, and commercial products — all competing for roughly the same early-adopter audience.

The naming pattern didn't help. AutoClaw, OpenClaw, KimiClaw, ArkClaw, QClaw, EasyClaw — these names blur together fast. When "ShogunAI" and "Project SKY" started circulating in tech Discord servers and comparison listicles, they sounded plausible. The naming conventions fit the category. The promises — zero-config setup, local processing, 24/7 autonomous operation — matched what real products in this space were actually shipping.

That's how phantom products get traction. A category becomes hot, the namespace fills with real products, and speculative or fictional names attach to the momentum. By August 2026, search volume for AI desktop agent comparisons had grown enough that content arbitrageurs began populating results with product names that hadn't shipped — or simply don't exist.

The timeline of verified products: AutoClaw (Zhipu AI) launched March 2026. EasyClaw (DocuAgile Pte. Ltd.) also launched publicly in early 2026. Both are verifiable. ShogunAI and Project SKY appear in no GitHub repository, no product changelog, and no verified press release as of today.

## What AutoClaw Actually Is

According to Skywork's AutoClaw review, AutoClaw is Zhipu AI's one-click desktop installer that packages the open-source OpenClaw framework for Windows and macOS. Installation completes in under 60 seconds — compare that to the 30 minutes to two hours required for manual OpenClaw setup.

The technical stack: Pony-Alpha-2 model (GLM-5 architecture, fine-tuned for agent tool-calling stability), with runtime switching to DeepSeek and other LLMs without reinstallation. It ships with 50+ pre-built skills covering content creation, code generation, financial analysis, and scheduling. All processing runs locally — no data transmitted to Zhipu servers during standard operation.

Pricing is straightforward: free installer, then approximately $12/month at 400 tasks, scaling to ~$47/month including optional DeepSeek credits. Users report saving approximately 8 hours weekly — a bold self-reported figure, but directionally consistent with what task automation delivers for repetitive workflows.

The limitation worth flagging: BestClaw's independent review scores AutoClaw 6.90/10.00, placing it at #12 on the 2026 unified leaderboard. It's tightly coupled to Zhipu's model stack and requires adapter layers for OpenAI or Anthropic integration. Not ideal for teams running multi-provider LLM strategies.

## What EasyClaw Actually Is

According to Skywork's EasyClaw analysis, EasyClaw is developed by DocuAgile Pte. Ltd. (Singapore) and positions as the only native GUI client for the OpenClaw framework. The installer runs under 200MB and deploys in under two minutes.

The differentiating feature: a vision-based AI engine that interacts with desktop UIs directly — clicking, form-filling, screen reading — without requiring application APIs. That's technically distinct from AutoClaw's approach. EasyClaw also routes across Claude, GPT-4o, and Gemini based on task type, and supports remote control via WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord, Slack, Feishu, and iMessage.

Pricing: Free tier (200 credits/day, 2,000 monthly limit), Plus at $20/month, Ultra at $200/month (44,000 monthly credits). The EULA falls under Hong Kong International Arbitration Centre rules, per the April 20, 2026 filing — relevant for enterprise legal review.

## Three Groups, Three Decisions

**Solo developers and researchers** running privacy-sensitive workflows should evaluate AutoClaw first. Local processing, a 60-second setup, and $12/month entry pricing make it the lowest-friction option. The 6.90/10 benchmark score suggests it's functional, not exceptional — good enough for personal productivity automation, not for production pipeline replacement.

**Small teams needing cross-platform automation** should look at EasyClaw. Multi-model routing across Claude, GPT-4o, and Gemini means you're not locked to a single provider's quality ceiling. The vision-based desktop interaction also opens use cases that browser-automation-only tools can't reach — automating a desktop application with no API and no web interface, for instance.

**Enterprise evaluators** should note that both products show meaningful gaps at scale. For enterprise-grade agent deployment, the headless AutoClaw variant (open-source, Docker-native, Kubernetes-scalable) or cloud-native competitors like ArkClaw from ByteDance are more appropriate starting points.

Two developments worth tracking: BestClaw's leaderboard updates quarterly, and EasyClaw hasn't received an official benchmark score yet — that changes the comparison picture once it does. Zhipu AI's adapter layer for OpenAI/Anthropic integration, if it ships, would close AutoClaw's biggest competitive gap.
